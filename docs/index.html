How it thinks

A chain of thought, as a path through memory.

An answer is a chain of facts. Ask a question and the search runs free, outward from what you named, along only the relations your question cares about. Hand it a claim to check and it digs from both ends until the tunnels meet. A link that looks too weak gets torn out and the chain is rebuilt.

step 1

Extract

Pull the facts that touch the starting situation and the request.

step 2

Weigh

Each edge gets a weight from its confidence and the question’s attention over relation types. Weak edges are never read.

step 3

Explore

A question: free search, outward from what you named. A claim: from both ends at once, until they meet. Each round touches only what changed last round.

step 4

Pick

Trust multiplies along the chain: every extra step costs a little, and two independent roads to the same answer beat one.

step 5

Repair

A link that looks too weak is banned and the whole chain is rebuilt; the better chain wins. On a graph with half its links missing this alone adds 7 to 16 points.

Every step costs trust. Two roads to the same answer earn it back.

We tried judging a chain by its weakest link, too. Its worst miss was three times bigger.

Not only the obvious links improved

Delete the fact you asked about, and mined rules still walk around the gap: on Wikidata5M the top answer stays right 56% of the time (67% with the fact in place, up from 45% and 54%). On the whole of Wikidata it found 314 rules that are right at least 99.9% of the time on facts hidden from them. Chains cross modules too: your memory says Tom lives in Lyon, Wikipedia says Lyon is in France.

It counts, compares and says no new

How many? Which is older? Both X and Y? Everyone besides X? Paris is to France as Rome is to what? What if Tom moved to Kyoto? Each question becomes a small program over the graph, every step shown. When nothing decides a yes or no, it says it can’t confirm. It never guesses.

Every fact knows where it came from

Text becomes facts without a model, with rules in 12 languages. Each fact keeps its document, segment and character span, so every answer can point back to the exact sentence it rests on.

Natural language in

Ask in plain words. It answers with the operations of thought.

A question arrives as a sentence. The Thinking Memory reader, not a parser but a reader that already holds the graph, turns it into a small program over that graph, composed from the atomic operations of human thinking, and every step of that program is shown with the answer. Atomic here means an operation that psychology measures as one step, with its own timing signature, error pattern or brain correlate. Larger acts are compositions of these.

Temporal memory

Always current. Never forgets what was true.

A new fact never overwrites an old one. It closes it, with a date. So the memory is always up to date, and you can still ask it as of any moment in the past: who held the post before, where someone lived last year, what you believed at the time.

Hardware

No GPU. No cluster. It runs on the CPU.

Graph search is matrix maths, but the matrix is almost all zeros: 2 cells in a million hold an edge. The fast way to multiply it is to skip the zeros and follow each active node to its neighbours. That work is too small and too scattered to fill a GPU; a compiled loop on an ordinary CPU does it. Every speed figure here was measured on a laptop, and the GPU stays free for the model.

Why the CPU wins here

The search, the strongest chain of facts with repair, runs as a compiled CPU loop: 0.10 ms a query, 0.155 ms with repair. It gives the same scores as the reference engine on every benchmark query we ran (3,464 on Wikidata5M, 600 on WikiKG90Mv2), and with repair on 299 of 300.
